Logistics

Phase 1 — ground roads & trucks
Tools
Industry
Storage tier for the next Mine/Mill/Town. Can't be resized later in Phase 1. A Mine always makes Ore; a Mill always turns Ore into Steel.
Terrain
Every cell has a terrain height, relative to an arbitrary 0 — shown as a faint tan (above 0) or blue (below 0) tint, with the exact level printed in its corner once it's non-zero. Ground/Elevated/Underground all follow it (Elevated is always one level above local ground, Underground one below), so raising or lowering a hill changes where its bridges and tunnels sit too. Requires the cell be completely clear of track and buildings first. Click a cell to raise or lower it by one level.
Network
The five-option dropdown applies to Road, Ramp, Tunnel Ramp, Connect, One-Way, Demolish, and — since rail also runs on all five layers — Build Track, Connect / Disconnect Track, and Toggle Signal Direction too. Ground/Elevated/Underground follow local terrain (see the Terrain section above); Deep Underground and Airspace are flat global layers that ignore it entirely — reserved for future modes (planes, mines) with their own way to reach them, so there's no ramp linking either to the rest of the network yet; road/rail can still be built there ahead of that. A Tunnel Ramp is different from a (bridge) Ramp: it doesn't link both grades at one cell, it slopes between two ADJACENT tiles — a ground tile and its underground neighbor one step over — so click the ground tile then the underground tile next to it (or the reverse order). It only works along a straight stretch: neither tile may have any other connection except the straight-through continuation, so a ramp never sits at a turn or junction. Two adjacent Ground/Elevated/Underground tiles also can't connect (auto- or manually) if their terrain differs by more than 1 level — shown as a dark cliff edge on the map. Uncheck auto-connect to lay road tiles side by side without joining them — handy for parallel one-way streets — then use Connect / Disconnect to wire up (or sever) specific edges by hand. Facing sets the single side of the next Station that can ever touch a road; a Mill needs two Stations — one handling Ore for delivery, one handling Steel for pickup.
Vehicles
A Bulk Truck can only ever be given Ore-handling Stations as stops; a Flatbed can only be given Steel-handling ones. The order editor enforces this — a mismatched stop is rejected.
Rail
A Rail Depot is a real loading platform: it must run alongside a straight, unbroken stretch of track along one of its long sides (highlighted in amber once built) — pick which way it's oriented and how long it is above; a longer platform needs a longer stretch of track waiting for it, but lets more of a long train dock (and load/ unload) at once. It works like a truck Station otherwise: build it touching an industry (or a chain of Stations reaching one) and trains load/unload that industry's own stock directly, same as a truck would. Left unlinked, a Depot instead buffers its own real storage. A longer train docked alongside more of the platform loads/unloads faster — transfer scales with how much of the train's own length actually overlaps the platform. Build a Station touching it for road access, same as a Mine or Mill.
A train isn't bought off the shelf — it's assembled at a Train Yard from one Engine (its physics: speed, mass, engine/brake force) and however many identical Wagons you pick (its cargo: capacity, and the one resource that Wagon type carries — same fixed-per-vehicle rule as a truck). Pick engine/wagon/count, then click a track tile touching a Yard to assemble and pay for the train there.
Game
Save downloads the whole world (buildings, vehicles, orders, rail block state, treasury) as a JSON file (§12). Load replaces the current world with a previously saved file — there's no undo, so save first if you want to keep what you have.
About
Trucks load and unload at Stations, never at a Mine, Mill, or Town directly. A Station must touch a Mine, Mill, Town, or another Station — chain Stations together to reach a road from an industry that isn't right next to one. An elevated road never connects to a ground road at the same cell — only a Ramp links the two layers. Trains work the same way but on their own network — track instead of road, Rail Depots instead of Stations. A signal is rail's one-way: it also marks a hard block boundary, so two trains can never occupy the same signaled segment at once. Rail also runs on ground and elevated layers, same as road — a Rail Ramp links them, entirely independent of the road Ramp, so a cell can have either, both, or neither. A train on elevated rail blocks elevated road traffic at a crossing exactly like a ground train blocks ground road traffic. Ground road and rail share the same grade (elevated road and elevated rail share theirs), so a road and a track can cross at the same cell — but only at a right angle (marked with a white X), like a real level crossing; a track running the same direction as an existing road there won't connect through it. Underground is a third grade for road and rail alike, drawn dashed and dimmed since it's beneath everything else — a Tunnel Ramp slopes down to it from an adjacent ground tile (not a same-cell link like a Ramp), and only ever along a straight stretch: no turns or junctions right at the ramp itself. There's no direct elevated-underground ramp — going from one to the other always passes through ground in between, one ramp at a time. Every cell also has a terrain height (Terrain section) — Ground, Elevated, and Underground all follow it, moving up or down together wherever the land does, while Deep Underground and Airspace are flat global layers, always the same height everywhere. Neither has a ramp connecting it to the rest of the network yet — they're reserved for future modes (airplanes, mines) that will reach them their own way. Two adjacent Ground/Elevated/Underground tiles can only connect if the terrain doesn't change by more than one level between them — too big a jump reads as a cliff, and needs terraforming (or a longer detour) before a road or track can cross it.
